# Shower Guava Freedom Act

**Proposed by Devin Lin**

**Citizen proposal — not introduced or enacted.**

**Draft version:** 1.0

**Document date:** October 18, 2026

**Publication date (America/Vancouver):** October 18, 2026

The Shower Guava Freedom Act is a proposed British Columbia statute protecting the personal choice to consume guava while showering in a private dwelling. It would prohibit the defined public authorities, when exercising powers under a Provincial enactment, from imposing or enforcing a prohibition, restriction or penalty solely because an individual makes that choice. The protection concerns guava, including portions of the fruit, and would apply whether a restriction refers to guava specifically or to food generally.

The defined public authorities are the government of British Columbia; municipalities, including the City of Vancouver; regional districts; and people exercising statutory powers on their behalf. The draft would expressly bind the provincial government.

Restrictions would remain permitted to the extent reasonably necessary to address a specific risk, supported by reasonable evidence, to health or safety, property, or the operation of plumbing or sewer infrastructure. The protection would take priority over conflicting regulations, bylaws, orders and administrative decisions under Provincial enactments, while preserving the authority of other Acts of the Legislature.

The draft would leave private property rights and lawful tenancy terms, strata bylaws and private contracts intact. It would provide no exemption from applicable federal law or liability for negligence or property damage. It excludes the default offence and penalty provisions identified in section 7 and relies on existing public-law remedies, without creating a new enforcement body. If enacted, it would come into force on Royal Assent.
